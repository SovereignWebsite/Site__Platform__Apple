Transcription on Apple devices

On this page

  • For developers: SpeechAnalyzer
  • Which devices
  • Languages
  • For people: Dictation, and Notes
  • What our own app uses
  • What Apple's pages do not say

Apple's developer pages say that with SpeechAnalyzer "you can bring advanced, on-device transcription to your app".[10] This page sets out what Apple's own pages say about it, and what they do not.

For developers: SpeechAnalyzer

  • Apple's WWDC25 session calls it "the next evolution of our speech-to-text API", introduced in iOS 26 and following the older SFSpeechRecognizer from iOS 10.[18]
  • It runs on the device: "transcription is entirely on device but the models need to be fetched".[18]
  • The model is kept in system storage, and does not add to the app's download size, its storage or its memory use. The system installs updates itself.[18]
  • Apple Intelligence is not required for transcription. In the session's demo it is used for a separate step, generating a title.[18]
  • An app can hold support for "a limited number of languages at a time". The number: not stated.[18]
  • Apple names these types: SpeechAnalyzer, SpeechTranscriber, DictationTranscriber and AssetInventory. DictationTranscriber supports the same languages, model and devices as the older on-device SFSpeechRecognizer.[18]

Which devices

The session says SpeechTranscriber "is available for all platforms but watchOS with certain hardware requirements". The hardware requirements: not stated.[18] Apple's machine learning page lists speech among features for iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro, Mac and Apple Watch, without versions.[10]

Languages

Apple says speech recognition covers "a variety of languages".[10] The session says the transcriber handles a set of languages "with more to come", and its transcript text does not give the list.[18] The list of languages: not stated.

For people: Dictation, and Notes

  • Apple's iPhone guide says Dictation is processed on the iPhone in many languages and needs no internet connection. Dictating into a search field may send the text to a search engine. These points come from a summary of the page.[19]
  • Better spelling, punctuation and capitalisation need Apple Intelligence and are described as English on newer iPhones. Basic Dictation does not. Also from the summary.[19]
  • Apple lists "Notes transcription summaries" for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision Pro, among features that work with iOS 26 and later.[3]
  • Dictation's own device and operating system requirements, and its language list: not stated.

What our own app uses

The team behind the VoiceDebrief iOS app reports that it uses SpeechAnalyzer to transcribe speech on the device, with no key and no server of ours. See Our own work.

What Apple's pages do not say

The hardware requirements, the languages, the number of languages an app may hold, and anything in the Speech documentation: not stated. Those documentation pages returned only their titles when we read them.
